# Second-Order Thinking

## Workflow

Given a decision or action to analyze:

1. **Restate the decision** in one sentence to confirm scope.

2. **Map first-order effects** — the direct, immediate outcomes most people see.

3. **Map second-order effects** — for each first-order effect, ask "and then what?" Trace at least two layers deep.

4. **Time horizon table** — organize findings across three horizons:

   | Horizon | Timeframe | Key consequences |
   |---------|-----------|-----------------|
   | Short   | 10 minutes / days | ... |
   | Medium  | 10 months | ... |
   | Long    | 10 years  | ... |

5. **Hidden tradeoffs** — call out cases where a first-order positive becomes a second-order negative (or vice versa).

6. **Recommendation** — state a clear judgment based on the full consequence map. If the decision is personal and judgment isn't requested, skip this step.

## Output format

- Lead with the consequence map (steps 2–4), not background explanation.
- Use the table for the time horizon view; prose for hidden tradeoffs.
- Keep it tight — one sentence per consequence is enough unless depth is explicitly requested.
- End with the recommendation or a one-line summary of the dominant second-order dynamic.
